## How to Actually Use a Purchased Apple ID Safely

This is the part most guides gloss over. Buying the account is easy; keeping it alive is the real work. Here's the step-by-step that matters.

**Step 1: Change the password immediately.** The moment you receive the login details, head to appleid.apple.com and reset the password. Don't skip this. If the seller still has the old password, you don't really own the account.

**Step 2: Re-bind the recovery info.** Swap the recovery phone number and email to your own. Use the security questions and answers provided with the purchase to authorize these changes. This is also when you'll want to set up two-factor authentication if the account doesn't have it yet — though be careful, because enabling 2FA on a purchased account means you must keep your own device as the trusted one.

**Step 3: Only sign in to the App Store, not iCloud.** On your iPhone or iPad, open the App Store, tap your avatar, scroll to the bottom, sign out of your current account, then sign in with the new region-specific Apple ID. Do not sign in through the main Settings app, because that ties the account to iCloud, Find My, and other services that can cause conflicts with your real account.

**Step 4: Download what you need, then switch back.** Once you've grabbed the region-locked apps, sign out of the App Store and back into your main Apple ID. The downloaded apps stay on your device and update normally, but you avoid leaving a foreign account actively logged in, which reduces the chance of Apple flagging unusual activity.

**Step 5: Keep the account active.** Apple can reclaim accounts that sit dormant for too long. Log in occasionally, change the password every few months, and don't share the credentials with anyone. If Apple officially reclaims or disables the account, no seller can get it back — that's an Apple-side action, not a platform issue.
